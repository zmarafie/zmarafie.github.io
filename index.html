Research

Most of my work applies artificial intelligence inside Internet of Things systems, where sensors, small computers and machine learning work together.

On the wrist

HeatWatch, current project

HeatWatch uses wearable sensors and machine learning to spot heat stress early and to alert people before it becomes heat illness. It is a current project with Dr. Shaikhah Alkhadhr at Kuwait University.

On the road

AutoCoach, from my PhD

AutoCoach is a driver feedback agent. It models each driver's personality and tailors its feedback, with the aim of improving driving behavior and road safety. I developed it with Prof. Kwei-Jay Lin at UC Irvine, and it is described in papers in Electronics and at IEEE SOCA.

Across the city

GreenScore

GreenScore is a sustainability assessment framework for IoT systems, part of my work on smart cities.

Next, I am extending this work into medical AI.

About

I am a Computing Professor and applied AI researcher specializing in the Internet of Things, intelligent systems, real-time computing, and the development of technology-driven solutions for real-world challenges.

My research focuses on translating emerging technologies into practical and impactful systems across digital health, smart environments, sustainability, mobility, and data-driven decision support. I am particularly interested in wearable sensing, edge intelligence, machine learning, personalized feedback systems, and the responsible use of AI in critical and socially relevant domains.

My current research includes wearable-based systems for monitoring heat-related health risks through real-time sensing and analytics, frameworks for evaluating the sustainability of IoT devices, and projects involving smart systems, health analytics, intelligent decision support, and real-time data processing.

I am also interested in the application of artificial intelligence and data analytics to real estate valuation and market decision-making, particularly within the context of the Kuwaiti property market.

My doctoral research at the University of California, Irvine, resulted in AutoCoach, an intelligent IoT system that analyzed driving behaviour and delivered personalized feedback to support safer driving.

Alongside my research, I am committed to mentoring students, supervising interdisciplinary projects, and building stronger connections between academia, industry, and entrepreneurship. I welcome research collaborations, industry partnerships, and innovation opportunities in applied AI, IoT, digital health, smart systems, sustainability, and intelligent data-driven platforms.

    Data Protection in AI

    Invited talk, 8 October 2024, at the conference Artificial Intelligence Between Law and Technology: The Role of Lawyers in AI and Data Protection, held by the Kuwait Lawyers Bar Society with the French Embassy in Kuwait.

    I covered the privacy and security risks of AI systems: collecting data without consent, anonymization that can be reversed, attacks that corrupt or trick AI models, bias, data crossing borders, and who is accountable when AI gets it wrong. I closed with security in the metaverse, the Internet of Things and self-driving cars.

    College news (Arabic)

    Applications of AI in the Legal Field

    Invited talk, 7 February 2024, in the second session of The Future of Digital Justice conference, held for Legaltech Day by the Kuwait Lawyers Bar Society with the Kuwait University Law Students Association and the KILAW Students Association.

    An introduction to AI for lawyers: what it is, how it developed, and how it already supports decisions in finance and healthcare. I then showed how law firms use it for legal research, contract review, predicting case outcomes, sorting documents for e-discovery, and client chatbots.

Posters

  • Nov 2025

    Plugged into the Planet: Measuring IoT Sustainability with GreenScore

    Z. Marafie, S. Alkhadhr

    A framework that scores how sustainable an IoT device is, based on its materials, how efficiently it runs and how much energy it uses. It then uses generative AI to suggest ways to improve the score. We showed it on everyday devices such as fitness trackers, smart thermostats and light bulbs.

    Poster4th Kuwait International Conference on Life Sciences (KICLS 2025), Kuwait University, 11 November 2025

    KICLS 2025Conference programme

  • Nov 2025

    Pulse of the Campus: Real-Time Health Tracking with Bangle.js 2

    A. Elshabrawi, J. Anjom, Z. Marafie, S. Alkhadhr

    A real-time system that streams step counts, heart rate and motion from a Bangle.js 2 smartwatch straight to a browser dashboard, with no app to install. It follows activity across the Kuwait University campus and adds campus-wide step challenges to encourage healthier habits.

    Poster4th Kuwait International Conference on Life Sciences (KICLS 2025), Kuwait University, 12 November 2025

    KICLS 2025Conference programme

  • Feb 2025

    HeatWatch: Real-time Detection of Heat Exhaustion via Wearable Sensors and AI Models

    Z. Marafie, S. Alkhadhr

    Machine learning on wearable readings, such as heart rate and movement, together with temperature and humidity, to flag the risk of heat exhaustion early. We tested it on simulated data modelled on Kuwait's climate; testing in real conditions is the next step.

    PosterPoster Day, Scientific Research Conference 2024 to 2025, Kuwait University
